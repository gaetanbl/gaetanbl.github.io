The GMA Experience

Even though my heart lies in robotics and AI, I chose to pursue a Mechanical and Control Engineering degree (GMA) at INSA Rennes. I truly believe that having a rock-solid engineering foundation helps me stand out from purely software profiles. The various internships I've completed capture the core spirit of GMA wonderfully.

At XSun, I tackled finite element analysis (FEA) and materials science. At ADDLAB (Decathlon), my focus shifted to advanced 3D modeling and additive manufacturing. During my time at Metallicadour (later acquired by Safran) and through my academic coursework, I got hands-on with the operation and control theory of industrial robotic arms. And of course, my latest R&D adventure at Scalian awaits you further down!

Beyond that, my curriculum gave me a deep appreciation for advanced control theory and physics—covering everything from Noise, Vibration, and Harshness (NVH) to advanced PID control concepts like soft sensors.

To cap it all off, my final year is all about international mobility and a graduation internship. After picking up a brand-new language from scratch in just a few months, I headed off to study in Brazil, diving headfirst into machine learning, advanced mathematics (Partial Differential Equations-PDEs), and coding!

AI Project #2

🤖 Duckiebot RL

During my R&D internship at Scalian, I developed and continuously refined an end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline to autonomously pilot a physical robot (a Duckiebot DB21J powered by an onboard Jetson Nano) using NVIDIA Isaac Sim, Isaac Lab, PyTorch (focusing on PPO), Docker, and ROS.

Along the way, I tackled classic hurdles like the Sim-to-Real gap, complex infrastructure setups, and reward hacking. One of my core milestones was implementing a custom CNN to radically upgrade how the robot perceives its environment. By leveraging an Actor-Critic architecture with a NatureCNN feature extractor, I watched yellow-line detection and overall driving smoothness improve dramatically. A particularly exciting breakthrough was stacking multiple consecutive frame inputs, allowing the model to naturally perceive velocity and acceleration rather than just static snapshots. This approach was directly inspired by the landmark research paper "Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning", where frame stacking was first used for Atari 2600 games because single frames couldn't capture motion trajectories.

The absolute highlight? Watching the physical robot execute complex maneuvers completely autonomously, driven purely by a policy learned in a GPU-accelerated simulator. Combining deep RL, 3D physics, embedded ROS, and MLOps made this one of the most thrilling chapters of my engineering journey so far!
